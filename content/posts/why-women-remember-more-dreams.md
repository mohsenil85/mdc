Women report remembering more dreams than men. It's one of the sturdier findings in dream research — when Schredl and Reinhard pooled roughly 175 studies, the pattern held. The gap opens around adolescence, runs widest in our twenties, and narrows again as we age.

## Hold it loosely

The gap is an average, not a rule. It's also small — plenty of men out-remember plenty of women, and if you wake with nothing most mornings you are well inside the ordinary range.

And gender does not fit into two boxes. [Our dreams are non-binary](/posts/non-binary-dreaming/) even when the research is binary, so take the whole finding with a grain of salt.

## It isn't the corpus callosum

I originally came across this claim in a book from the eighties, *Women's Bodies, Women's Dreams* by Patricia Garfield. It stated that women have a larger corpus callosum — the band of fibers joining the two hemispheres — and that this is why we dream more richly, or intuit more easily, or feel more fluently.

That claim comes from a single 1982 paper that measured fourteen brains. When a later review pooled some fifty studies, the difference dissolved once head size was accounted for. Men have a larger corpus callosum in absolute terms because they have larger brains. Measured against total brain volume the picture flips, but that's a fact about scaling rather than about sex — bigger brains of any kind have proportionally smaller callosa.

It's a tidy story. It simply isn't true. And split-brain patients, whose corpus callosum has been surgically cut, still dream.

## What does predict recall

Three things, none of them glamorous.

**Waking briefly in the night.** You can only file a dream if you surface while it's still there. People who recall a great deal wake more often in the night than people who don't — which is also why recall runs richest in the luteal phase, when [many of us are already surfacing more](/posts/we-dont-dream-like-men/).

Activity in the temporoparietal junction, the region where the sense of a self located in space gets built. High recallers show more blood flow there, asleep and awake.

**Being interested.** The most boring finding in the literature and the strongest one. Attention is most of it.

## So why us?

Nobody knows. But it is an interesting prompt.

Maybe it's the light sleeper: a body built to wake for an infant wakes more often, and every surfacing is another chance to carry something back. Maybe it's social rehearsal — if dreaming practices relationships, whoever held the group together had more to practice. Maybe it's a threat drill, and different bodies braced for different dangers. Or maybe dreams just ride along on a memory system that already recalls waking life with more scene and more feeling.

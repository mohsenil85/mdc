Dream Journal

Ten days camping in the mountains with my three-year-old.

(Yes, you read that right.)

1

Somewhere in there I noticed my dreams had changed.

More vivid. And I was waking with them still intact —

not fragments, but whole dreams, easy to reach.

2

Maybe it was sleeping closer to the moon.

I’d like that to be the answer.

But I think it was something much more ordinary.

3
What actually happened

My toddler slept in. So I woke slowly, to birdsong and sunlight, and then I simply lay there.

No phone to reach for. No breakfast to make yet. Nothing asking anything of me.

4

And my dreams were right there.

5

I don’t think I was dreaming more.

I think I was finally leaving enough room to notice.

6

Most mornings, the moment we open our eyes we start doing — breakfast, the toddler, the phone before we’ve fully sat up.

The part of us that plans and sorts comes online fast, and it crowds out the part that was still dreaming.

7

The dream doesn’t slip away because it wasn’t important.

It slips away because nothing made space for it.

8
Bring it home

You don’t need ten days in the mountains. You need a few minutes.

Stay where you are. Eyes closed. Don’t chase the dream — chasing scatters it. Let it come back to you.

It takes patience. We’re used to quick answers. This is slower.

Dream Journal

We don’t dream like men.

This became obvious the moment I started weaving the body into my dreamwork.

1

We have monthly cycles that shift our hormones — and shift our dreams.

We can gestate humans, which changes our sleep and the makeup of our nights.

Postpartum. Perimenopause. Menopause.

Each one a portal that changes how we sleep and how we dream.

2
A little hormone literacy

Estrogen rises through the first half of your cycle

and peaks around ovulation.

It keeps things plump and outward-turned — more social, more engaged, more accommodating.

3

After ovulation, the body makes progesterone —

literally
“pro-gestation.”

The hormone that readies and holds the womb lining for a possible pregnancy. If no pregnancy comes, both hormones fall — and that drop is what brings the bleed.

4

When estrogen falls — premenstrually, and again in perimenopause —

we become less willing to people-please, or to put up with the same levels of nonsense.

Kate Codrington calls this the lifting of
the “veil of estrogen.”

5
Luteal phase

The stretch before your period

You wake more often through the night.

The more you surface from sleep, the more dreams you carry back with you.

Recall tends to be at its richest.

6
Follicular phase

After you bleed

Sleep tends to be sounder and less broken.

More time in REM, when dreams arrive especially vivid.

Fewer dreams remembered — but the ones that land, land hard.

7

Part of the practice is simply tracking this for yourself.

Your quality of sleep, and your dream recall, across the different parts of your month.

You may be surprised how much of a pattern there is.

8
Dreaming with the cycle, not against it

Late luteal and menstrual days — when reserves are low and the nervous system sits closer to the edge —

are not the days to plunge into your hardest material.

Follicular and ovulatory days can hold more.

9
🌙

Not one more thing to optimize —

a way of meeting yourself where you actually are.

Dream Journal

97%

of what exists is dark to us.

We can’t sense it. We can’t measure it. We don’t understand it.

1

“Physics estimates that 97 percent of what exists is called dark matter and dark energy. Dark, meaning we in our human form cannot tap into it.”

“So we live in bodies that allow us, in this form, to make sense of 3 percent of reality.”

— Dr. Dan Siegel

2

3%

That’s the slice our senses are built to register.

Everything else moves through us and around us — unfelt, unmeasured — while we go on making breakfast.

3

I don’t find that depressing.

I find it enormously relieving.

4
The filter is doing its job

To make sense of anything, we filter — dividing experience into categories and concepts.

That isn’t a flaw. It’s what lets you cross a street, hold a conversation, get a toddler into a car seat.

5

But the filter is set for waking life.

Calibrated for tasks, for language, for the parts of experience that can be sorted into words.

6
At night

The categories loosen.

Time stops behaving. People arrive as two people at once. The dead show up and it isn’t strange.

Whatever the day-mind was sorting into boxes gets handed back to you unsorted.

7

I’m not saying dreams give you access to dark matter. That’s not a claim I can make.

What I am saying is that the aperture opens — and what comes through arrives in a language older than words.

Which is why so much of a dream is felt before it’s understood.

8

Dreams widen the three percent.

Which is why we don’t rush them. Anything that snaps the filter back into place on waking closes the opening.

9
🌙

You’re not retrieving a file.

You’re keeping a wider aperture open a little longer.

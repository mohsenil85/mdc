Dream Journal

How to do dreamwork on your own.

You don’t need someone else in the room. Some of the deepest work I’ve done with my own dreams happened alone, in bed, before the house woke up.

1

First: put the dream dictionary down.

A book can tell you a snake means transformation. It can’t tell you what your snake means, in your body, on this particular morning.

That’s the only meaning that will do anything for you.

2
Before bed

Try some dream incubation.

A formal-sounding name for something simple. You ask. Put a question to the night — not a demand, an invitation:

What message am I hoping for?

What am I trying to process?

Where do I need guidance?

3
On waking

Don’t move yet.

Stay where you are. Eyes closed. No phone, no getting up, no starting the list.

Let the dream come back on its own — in pieces, out of order. A color before a room before a face.

4
On the page

Write it down badly.

Speed over quality. Fragments are fine. Present tense keeps you inside it:

I am standing in my grandmother’s kitchen and the water is rising.

Give it a title. “The Barn Is On Fire.”

5
In the body — first

Start with your associations.

What’s happening in your life? What’s unresolved, what you avoided, what you’re dreading or hoping for.

Then widen out — where are you in your greater cycles?

6

Somatic work starts with two questions, in this order:

What am I feeling?

Where is that in my body?

Throat, chest, belly, jaw, hips, the backs of your legs.

7

It is most likely a whisper.

Not a shout. A slight tightening. A small warmth. A heaviness you’d walk straight past if you weren’t listening for it.

8
The question I come back to most

How would you explain this dream without words?

If you had to get it across using only your body — a gesture, a posture, the shape your hands make — what would you do?

9

Think dream-time charades.

Then actually do it. Alone in your room, no one watching. Or with a partner over morning coffee. Move it.

It feels silly, but the results are fascinating.

10
Know when to stop

If a dream floods you — shaking, numb, spun out, somewhere far from the room — stop. Feel your feet. Name what you actually see. The dream will keep.

Recurring nightmares and dreams tied to trauma are not solo material.

That’s not a failure of your practice. It’s the point where a nervous system needs company.

11
🌙

A single dream is interesting.

Thirty days of dreams is a map.
